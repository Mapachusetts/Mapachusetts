Mapachusetts

Politics, mapped to where you live. An interactive 3D globe that shows every layer of government covering a spot, plus the races and candidates that matter there.

Built with Kiro
The problem

Where you live decides who represents you. That is hard to see.

  • One address can sit in a congressional district, a state senate district, a state house district, a county, a town, a school district and a ward. Each layer has its own officials and its own races.
  • Voters rarely see all of those layers in one place.
  • Good, cited information about candidates is scattered across news sites and campaign pages.
2
The solution

Pick a place on a globe. See the whole stack.

  • Find a place four ways: search, Use my location, browse menus (State, County, City), or click the globe.
  • A layer dock of color-coded chips, each with a glowing outline on the map.
  • Ballot by layer: what each level of government does and the races it decides.
  • An Events tab for town halls and meetings, and an Election Day countdown.

Candidate research

Click a candidate. Get a cited, six-tab profile.

Six tabs

  • In the News (cited)
  • Funding
  • Career
  • Issues, with voting records
  • Sources
  • Myths & Facts

Real Massachusetts 2026 races

  • Governor: Maura Healey, Mike Minogue
  • U.S. Senate: Ed Markey, John Deaton
  • Every profile has a shareable link

Built to be checked

  • News summaries carry numbered citations
  • Citations are validated when the data is built
  • Only headlines, links, snippets and summaries are stored

How it works

Real boundaries, real research, one data seam.

Globe app

  • MapLibre 3D globe, OpenFreeMap basemaps
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb for districts, counties, towns and precincts
  • Ward data for Boston, Chicago and NYC
  • Photon geocoder for search
  • No API keys needed

Research app

  • Next.js (App Router) and Supabase
  • RSS news ingest into Supabase
  • Claude-written summaries with checked citations
  • Built for Vercel and Supabase hosting

One source of truth

  • Candidate data lives in the research app
  • A script exports it for the globe
  • Both apps show the same candidates
6
What is real, and what is not

We label our data honestly.

  • Real: Census boundaries, and the Massachusetts 2026 governor and U.S. Senate races.
  • Sample: other states' races, local offices and events. They are labeled "Sample" in the app.
  • U.S. only for now. Everything else on the globe is greyed out.
  • Districts and precincts can lag reality, so the app tells people to confirm with their local election office.
